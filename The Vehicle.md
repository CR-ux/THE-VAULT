

While on the vehicle, I would be told that I needed to return to earth for some unknown purpose. 
The vehicle came close to Earth once more, and we all tried to wave to the inhabitants, not realising they could not see us, but only the vehicle itself. Gazing in awe at the salvation not afforded to them. 
The vehicle found its way to my hometown, coventry, and I used my Will to resist its descent into an underground cathedral spire. 
i knew, somehow within this dream-truth, that what lied beneath, hiding inside of that black abyss, that inward drilled tower, was of a great and mighty darkness. 
I managed somehow to prevent the vehicle making its descent. 
The force, or forces, who had deemed it necessary for the vehicle to make this journey into the depths, protested, silently, and yet, the silence resonated with a heavier will than I could ever muster. 
Still, against this Goliath, my David-heart pulled, crying, in knowing, that I needed to protect those countless fellow passengers.
But something in me knew, in my holding, even though I could keep holding on, keep the vehicle stationary, it was no good. This, for some reason unbeknownst to me, needed to happen. And my stopping it, delaying it, was only holding back the sequence of events to which none of us could ever consent. Not really. 
Consent, to these beings, to this almighty darkness, was not relevant, it was simply not a compatible concept, not belonging in the same range of causal space. It was not a nefariousness that this Force propagated, these forces teemed, droned and hummed with (but which pronoun was it I could accurately assign? Plurality and singularity, too, seemed entirely nonsensical to ascribe it, or them, irrespectively). It was a sense of foreboding inevitability. An eminently imminent, immense immanence. 
Perhaps they were God, but more likely just the life that we are, at a different scale. Performing a function as mundane as feeding themselves, their children. To them, perhaps we were simply nutrients, passing through their digestive system. Perhaps we were forgotten the moment we were pulverised into a homogenous mulch and swallowed. Perhaps the cathedral spire was an oesophagus. Perhaps our death, our terror or our looming demise, of which the other passengers were seemingly unaware, was simply the monotonous groan of this entity's bowel. Perhaps the memory of our entire sacred lives, the endlessly knotted meanings we have so painfully gathered, like humble sticks of crackling firewood, were simply shit being wiped off a toilet bowl.


After this, resistance to, and incredible control of the vehicle; I was told, or assumed, that I would be let back, that this was a return mission. That I had done well, shown potential for the progression of our species. I had made Some Body proud.

But in reality, I had failed them, with my Will.
With my opacity, I'd become a window
Into everything they couldn't be. 
I had been sent back 
to perish with the other 
remaindered masses.
