Semiotics 

Derrida's concept of différance explains that meaning is not fixed within a word or sign but arises from a complex system of differences and the ongoing deferral of ultimate meaning. The word itself, by subtly altering the French "différence" (difference), plays on its identical pronunciation with "différance" to convey its dual meaning: to differ (how signs are distinct from each other) and to defer (how meaning is never fully present but continually postponed). Therefore, the meaning of any sign is not self-contained but is always defined by its relationship with other signs, a dynamic process that is essential to language and understanding but perpetually shifts and never reaches a final, stable state. Derrida builds on the ideas of Ferdinand de Saussure, who argued that meaning in language comes from the differences between words rather than their intrinsic qualities.
