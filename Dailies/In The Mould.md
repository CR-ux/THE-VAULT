I told my dad that I was doomed here, that I'd die. He still got out of the car, leaving me there in the passenger seat, to eat something. To die.
I thought about driving away, and as I thought about driving, it was suddenly happening. 
And I found myself on an exceedingly tall hill 
at the top of this hill was a gargantuan generator, tired with bearing the enormity of the electricity it provided to the houses at the bottom of the hill. The same house where I, along with a group of nameless others, had been massacred over a month prior. 
And atop this hill was a sewage pipe, empty of waste, but fertile with a stunning slime mould, in the same palette as my paintings of Lambs. 
I had discovered on some mysterious website, a video of my naked body, greyed and blackened with sewage and soot, climbing out of this pipe. 
The video was terrifying, my limbs multiplying, my body not unlike that of an insect, but with decidedly more flesh. Decaying, frantically cracking and flailing as it climbed its way out. I was smiling. And the smile had now turned to a hazy, waxy slime. 
I was trying to convince the friend I was with, who had returned from the place from which they had came, who was perhaps an angel, or who was perhaps an alien entity-- perhaps they are one and the same-- that I had seen this video. That it really did exist. That it had terrified me, enthralled me, and that the composition it accompanied was my best work, But now, it had disappeared, and I was unable to verify my claims. 
Instead, this being was poking this slime mould, fascinated at the layers of sheen they were scraping from the cylinder of concrete. 

My father had returned by this time, and was on the phone, to some unknown AI, or perhaps a conglomerate of angels, or perhaps an alien entity -- perhaps they are all just one and the same. 
I was pretending to be asleep, and was revealing, in spite of himself, that he had been the first source of infidelity in my parents' marriage. 
This revelation, made by him, through them, became mine -- perhaps we are all one and the same -- and I had betrayed my own diguise; rousing in my waking sleep, both in this world, and in this one. 
He quickly spluttered something akin to "isn't it weird how these things get you to admit things you haven't done?" as the phone signal clicked and popped in time with the generator behind him. He was checking if I was indeed awake, and if I'd finally found out that all of his years of turmoil, his violence and entitled vitriol; had been just that -- an excuse to take out his wasted life on his family. To turn all of our greying memory into a coat of slime. 

There was a beautiful house on the other side of the hill; in a deep palette. The bay windows jutting out, were two hexagons, split in half. Somehow I knew the inside, from that distance, even from there I knew there were only Six Rooms.

-- this place I go when I sleep, it holds such depths of hue -- this dilapidated mansion was sighing under its own weight, destined for collapse. But in its little time left, it had been seen by someone, perhaps for the first time, who truly understood what it meant. For I knew what this house meant, not in its breath, but in what it meant to me, and maybe it just needed to know that it could mean something, through someone. 

I tried to tell my father, and the angel, and the many lost lives, living and dead, all lost, buried at the bottom of the hill, going about their suburban dreariness, or still in the graves I had once shared with them, where I couldn't find my shoes; and after my feet had stopped needing them. After I had stopped waiting for the gun.
I tried to tell them all what beauty lay on the other side of that great mound, but they were too rapt in their respective tasks of scraping what was then hidden, and hiding what is now scraped, to hear me. 

There were clothes blowing in a bellowing wind silent, somehow stronger in its silence -- the unseen force promising the house's end, and suggesting my own. 

The laundry bungeed back and forth, like a spider at the whims of such a gale, and I noticed a dress above me, subjected to the same fate. 

I tried to grab the dress as it came closer, over the dizzyingly steep canyon and towards the perch the three, or one of us found ourselves upon. As I grabbed it, trying to pull it from its pegs, in order to save it, the wind pulled me back with it, and I was certain I would fall to my death, no plastic to hold me to the thread of the living. 

And there, then my mother arrived. 
My mother arrived and I realised all the villains my heart has made for her in waking, in sleep, in the wake of the sleep of my childhood's artifice. The childhood I can barely remember, and the childhood I will spend the rest of my adulthood convincing myself was full of such grasping, clawing hateful glare, and searing fear, now settled, fettered into moulding glaze. 

My mother tried the best she could, while telling herself, and us, she didn't. So that we might convince her otherwise. And my father told himself, and us, he did, perhaps to be proven wrong. Perhaps they are all the same. 

Again, there, I found myself trying to convince her, at the brink of falling to the abyss, that I was terrified of heights, that I felt something. And there, once again, amidst these angels, these devils of the past, these artificial and extraterrestrial clones, nobody believed me. Atop this hill, amidst the sameness: of the spark of life, and the dark of waste,  I had known who I was, through the ignorance of all around me. 

And perhaps that's all knowing oneself is, a summit, mound, island, standing all alone. Perhaps it is the unawareness of those who should have seen, that really is the light, the forgotten memories, coating passageways of emptiness, the skin that coats the exit of the waste, the teetering tatters of animal hide to coat our skin, the hiding of the fall of man, and the house's shutters, shuddering--

I know that they are all the same.
