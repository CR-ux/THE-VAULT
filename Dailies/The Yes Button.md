## LAST NIGHT'S DREAM

in the dream, i was on a pier, with my friend. she told me we were going treasure hunting in a scrap heap. i saw gigantic lilies reaching to the sunset; and i told her that we must have them. she agreed. 
eventually within the dream the lilies became irrelevant, as we entered a row of quaint restaurants, with cafeteria seating, rows upon rows of families eating together. 
on one of the rows was a girl, holding a keyboard, with her finger pressed on a single button, unable to tear it away. 
i asked at the counter for a drink, and was told its price, but even being so humble, i realised i couldn't afford it; and so i stood there and waited, pretending to watch the information video about it. 
i finally plucked up the courage to tell him i'd been wasting his time, and that my staying there was simply for embarrassment; i'd been wasting both of our time at the same time. 
i got back to the table with the girl, where my friend now sat, and i tried to let the girl know that she didn't need to keep her finger holding down the button. 
she wouldn't listen, and instead seemed incredibly distressed by the matter, so i thought, wrongly, in the dream, that perhaps it had fallen to me to help show her that nothing would go wrong if she'd stop. 
so i took the keyboard from under her. 

it was not the button, upon which was simply written the word 'yes'
but rather, the belief in the button, which demanded that she indeed keep pressing it. 
her eyes took on a horrendously terrifying appearance, grotesque and sinister; becoming the shape of a bridge reflected in water. the eyeballs sinking back into the sockets, and the eyelids curling outwards; and the girl making the most disturbing, grumbling, gurgling, creaking and snapping sounds. 
and this is how her belief in the button led to my belief in the button. 

but moniker, who, at some point had left the place, within the dream, was not afflicted as such. i remember her telling me something to do with thought, the power of it, and the power that our thoughts' power has on the thoughts of others, and subsequently, their power. 
My friend, in waking life, was on the other side of the room, but i knew that she wouldn't come to my aid, because she didn't believe in the button; and to come over would be to give the whole affair the power that would kill us both. 
i was paralysed, in the dream, 
and in the wake of it;
so in my stone bodied stillness i tried to scream the name "ARIA", 
who, as it happens, is a character in my longest story. 
she never came. even in the story of the dream, i knew that my power of thinking this story into being came not from me, but from the story itself; aching its way into existence, by evading awareness of its punishment. only seeding the idea of it. and the threat-- the threat that, maybe not writing it could lead to punishment-- allowing this to fester, until the only thing left is to be enslaved by its genesis. 


i tried, desperately, to give the keyboard back to the girl. 
she was too far gone, far too gone into this gore of tentacles, abysmal, deep abysmal eyes, and teeth. sharp teeth, chattering. spitting out some sort of indecipherable sound -- perhaps she was calling out the name of one of her characters. 

i tried, desperately, to give the keyboard back to whatever was left of the girl. 

she couldn't recognise it anymore, wherever she was now; for she wasn't here. 
she had believed, and i had taken that belief--
i had taken that belief and now i was stuck with it--

and i knew then what fiction meant
