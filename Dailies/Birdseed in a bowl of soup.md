

My next painting came to me in this dream.
I was painted into it -- 
I was not in the subject of the painting, but of me, was that painting. And my composition glowed, dark, weighted with the deepest hues. 
Dear god it was so intimate-- such rations of light, so starved of it-  beautiful. 
And in my Edinburgh flat, recently devoid of my abuser, my familiar revealed that he was actually a crustacean with a poisonous stinger.  I was so proud of him, for accepting his repulsive nature; for scaring all my birthday party guests away. 
And outside, wondering the city into being, wandering through the I, that had once been raptured by terrorism; by mirrors and the terror of reflection, under shelter of cold concrete I met the woman, gaunt, bound by an exhausted smile; who I repeatedly see within my dreams. And there, on the doorstep where she had been sleeping I told her: “you’re everywhere; you follow me everywhere — are you from that place?"
She nodded. 
I told her that was where I grew up. I asked: "Are you following me? Into all my dreaming?” 
She had offered no direct reply. 
Instead, she told me the heavy rocking cradle of agony; the rolling ocean of her shouldered pain: she could never shake the idea of ending her life. 
I told her, with such unrecognisable conviction, in that dream-- I was just so irrevocably sorry, because I knew, I have only ever known how suffocating is this suffering. How ever present is that Great Looming Question. But I had continued, somehow, I continued to let her know my caring, let us just know the heaving truth -- that I can’t do it. We could no longer carry-- that surely, sure now, that I have to, and have, I have to have let it go now, beaten from its bars-- the geotropic miscarriage of hope of the idea; of letting go. 
It was a birdseed, that boulder.
And I woke up; still asleep I woke up, and now upon waking again, for the first time, I knew that she was suicide.

The closing sequence of this opened box, where the dream ended, was where the painting began. 

I was just outside of the flimsy ribs of a rusted birdcage; pitiful. A wake of bird alighted-- I wake to write this and realise, I must have been a lighted bird. 

And striking impossible as the inevitable lightning's return; I began that eternal pilgrimage, of an eye traversing the memory of a painting. 
I was the eye of a bird performing the impossible task of backward flight, with no thought of gravity, or as the wind against its wing--
And simply looking on, with no wonder, but detached understanding of the light and the mystery of magnets--
I began to leave that place with my eye upon the fledgling stagelamp, caressing down upon the top of the bars; 
And further back I drew, past row behind row of theatre seating; emptied of bodies
The greens! The dying peacock greens of the scene, they were oceans unto themselves; and the blacks were blood, a beetle shell in the night--
The light shared between so many forms that it grew thinner, thinner, disappearing, as I reached the upper circle, and exited the place, eyes still fixed on this spotlit cage, that which I had once relied on for rest-
And through a theatre box, a pinhole now, that metallic structure alone in the centre of that stage --
but still, still visible;

And into the back of a humble restaurant, also empty of patrons; 
Quaint, quieted, asleep yet breathing steady in potential--

my pace slowed as I reached the clear glass window that revealed my all--

And I stopped there, that stage lamp a star; I was seeing the star that the nucleus of that cage had become; waiting for the next day; where the curtain beckoned, unknown to most, at the edge of a universe, contained within the fleeting warmth, of a bowl of soup, between the mismatched cutlery, on a paper-clothed and temporary family table, where hungry mouths may hear the words of their waitress, letting them know, in the drying walls, of the tired, chipping edges, of a plastered smile--
There, the whispering promise of an audience in the background:

The show was about to begin.
