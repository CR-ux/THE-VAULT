On Dreaming
---

in the dream, i was in an apartment not unlike the one from which i write about it
my grandmother had come to visit, and i seemed to be quite wrapped up in the reality of the dream; as in waking life, i'd become so absorbed within the process of experience that I'd apparently forgotten it was a fantasy.
i felt grateful that i had tidied my place somewhat, as I instantly recognised her voice in the hallway. 
i looked through the peephole and sure enough, it was her. 
She mentioned she had no additional clothes to the outfit she was wearing, and that her legs were made to appear significantly skinnier in these denim jeans. She was right -- she looked like what she was: a cartoon from my childhood. 

i had been told that my familiar, a ferret, was an animatronic doll, that his nature was unnatural, that he was a mass produced toy, with built in stink functionality-- I remember thinking, why would people elect for this, in the name of accuracy? And now, in shame, I recall that this cheapened him in a certain sense, it brought our bond into question -- who was he to me if he was made for this, made to be a toy? What was the point if he didn't have free will, and only served one purpose?

One by one, my entire family, and my rapist, somewhere I am sure, maybe both, and all, but maybe one but estranged from me, and then, but now, now I think I'm now I am here but am I? I was but then there began the entering of the space I wrongly called my own, my body. My mother was introducing me to my dead grandfather. He showed me a letter he'd written, presumably from the Other Side, and I was shocked at the similarity of our styles of calligraphy. So I began searching for my Green Book, to show him a particular page it reminded me of. 

Thematic of my dreams, the foraging was fruitless, futile; but still, I kept searching. Maybe everyone is dead. Maybe everyone I see is dead and I never lived at all.

In my search, the countless bodies bound in blood were assuming roles, small stations of scenes, each of them more horrifying than the last. I couldn't die or be there, living in the ways they needed.

It dawned upon me that I was inside of a work of interactive media -- still apparently unaware of the dream I was trapped within. Or perhaps, more likely, entirely lucid. 

The first run through the series of stations of events, permutations of family members shooting, stabbing, manipulating, raping, tricking one another into winning, I was too terrified to claim my own agency in the process, and the 'machine', the system or the overarching player of this game (who in this first instance, was not I) was making the decisions, or rather passively selecting the sequence of events unfurling before me to some default, more terrifying and dangerous option at each of the binary junctures of the decision tree mapped out before me. Two choices, and at once, just one. Only ever two at once.

My apartment had, in the process, transformed into a labyrinthine system of cells, with no doors, but separated, in a similar fashion to office cubicles. It afforded the 'players' (or rather, the characters of my childhood) the chance to hide behind corners from their respective assailants, or to lay in wait for their next, assigned victim. Who or what was assigning these two 'teams', or how many 'teams' of two, or what the win condition of the game was for each character, I did not and still do not know. 

I do not have a brother, not in this world. But in that one, I did. And in that one, in the middle of the action, in our own flimsy facade of privacy, we had sex two times.

I realised this brother of mine was one of a set of twins. I was not his twin, for he was identical. he was one of two. Both of the brothers were of two, in their own respective bands of two, their respective bandmates a set of twins, too -- the two bands' names were 'Burned Economy' and 'Petroleum Jelly'.

After dying countless times within this system of hellish games between relatives, bodies strewn everywhere, I had begun to get a hang of the procedure; to know certain weak points of characters, to ascertain slightly more who was on my 'side', which really, just meant a state of neutrality. Which really, just meant 'not an immediate threat'. I began to realise who I was supposed to kill; and I began to hear the voiceover, asking me which option, of two, to take.  

Eventually, one by one, just as they had originally entered, as in, to two, the other characters, too, began to realise the agency, or lack thereof, they had in these events. Hessian bags were placed over heads of hostages to avoid seeing eyeballs fall out of their two sockets, a culmination of the action came to fore, and I found myself advancing in a small group against a wall of finalists, akin to a judges panel. I did not recognise anybody on either of the two sides of that war. And I still did not know, and didn't seem to ask, the motivation behind all of this horror. 

But there were no teams; not really. Everybody was out for themselves.
Every One just trying, in their own way, with their own pre-assigned set of rules, received from some mysterious second face, unknown and decidedly malevolent -- or worse, indifferent -- source, a mirror: to survive.
