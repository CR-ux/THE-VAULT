## LAST NIGHT'S DREAM
The Second Earth
in the dream, i was waking up.
i was waking up late, in the dream, on holiday. with my family. in the same place we'd gone every year, in the same place i seem to keep returning to in sleep. 
my family were there with me, but so were the family of my childhood friend. 
all this keeps barrelling towards a truth i am reaching for -- something terribly monstrous, a useless tautology about young childhood allied friends' origin, their parents, starred and featuring utterly, heavily in my dreaming sleep.

i had woken up inside this dream, and my sister was packing her belongings in her suitcase.
this alerted me, as always was and is the case, in waking in sleep and in sleeping wakefulness and wake of sleep awake, that i had forgotten to pack mine. so i began the necessary task, haphazardly. 
i was searching for my familiar, bassoon luis borges, around the apartment we'd been staying in. it was in that place. <font size="1.5">that place none of us go anymore. </font>

we were all evidently, in the dream, perhaps not only in the dream, waiting for transport to take us back home, and its arrival was apparently imminent. 
eventually i had found borges and was placing him into a bag so he could be safe and comfortable. 
*as i write this i hear him stirring from his sleep to nibble a toy and let me know he is here.* 

i stepped outside, inside the dream, and there were great lakes of infinite reflection, staring back at us through the stars of the endless night. 

there was such a beauty encapsulated in these waters that it was terrifying. 

at first we, or i, or the i that has split into this 'we' i was dreaming within, thought the moon had grown to a gargantuan enormity 
but the moon as we knew it had ceased to exist, and we were faced with the selves we distanced ourselves from. We were peering, not up, but across, toward a celestial tautology.
it was a second Earth; 

And all of them were terrified, for what this meant--
Who lived on this Earth? How had it sprung into existence as we slept?

I was not so afraid, though I felt enough fear for wariness, for enough wariness for enough empathy for their fear. 

the cars were arriving in a trickle, as drool on a pillow, and the others had become convinced that their insufficient numbers could only mean that the second Earth was heralding the ending of ours; that somehow we had been siphoned into newer, better versions of ourselves; that all the resources of the place we'd called home, before we'd fallen asleep, were fading like the memory of the dreams we'd once had for it.

and my stepsister, only for looking at my worry, and the worry metastasising across that two metre plain between us, neither belted or in seats, but a silence of scurrying, blown about inside a thin, flimsy vehicle, like leaves crisped to skeletons, in a groundskeeper's binbag, futile collections of selfsame remnants of selfhood; promising futile escape. escape not for longing, but for lack of space.

and then the bus came to a galvanised halt-- and his body was upturned onto me, beneath my stepsister, bent neck, motionless, i heard a crack, like lightening. but smaller. infinite. 

he lay there, on his back, for quite some time, while i sat just as motionless, only able to move my hand, through some sort of sleep paralysis not born from somnolence but from shock-- to check if the innocence of his ribcage still danced with the necessity of his lungs
i could not tell and i could not look down
