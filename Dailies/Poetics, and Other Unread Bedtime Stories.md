in the dream, i was in my father's bedroom -- not my mother's, not anymore, or even then, or ever, really, but mine 
And Borges had scratched a hole in the door, leaving a smaller one inside, that opened inward, even as the greater closed, or opened, out-

My sister was in the room, and wouldn't stop quoting Aristotle
I was growing angry at her, all I wanted was sleep, but she wouldn't stop reciting Aristotle 

I was so tired and I just wanted to sleep but my sister would not stop reading the ancient words of Aristotle
I need to call her back 

I don't know why I was so angry at her

In sleep, I needed to sleep--
Sleep not sufficient in its rest 
I am being told to write by five pigeons at my window 
Remembering all those gargantuan words my sister was reading at the threshold of that torn down doorway my sister would not stop speaking the words of Aristotle 
Her friend's mother has died and I still have not called her back 

I landed on my laptop where I'd wake to write these words and it had broken then, and I blamed her--
If she'd just given me a minute's quiet I might have heard it as a bedtime story 

But a bedtime story isn't for a sister to read, 
and I should have called her back 

yesterday, or the day before, or all the days before I was sure I would die 
And i still did not call her back 
I don't think I even thought of her 
I wrote my will into my paintings 
And i never called her back, or anyone 
I only thought of leaving some cryptic things behind 
To leave a puzzle, as a portal 
To leave some sense of poetry 
To make this fledgling whisper of a life a poem 

I think of my dead friend and how I cannot call them back 

I think of the gratitude of the Sea and I think of my mother 
and I think of a poem and all the poems I have ever written 
I think of how I told my friend just yesterday how nothing is ever happening 
How I keep dying and yet no life rises from the sludge 
How my organs are all tearing and I cry in Wells 
And I cannot feed my children 
I think of the Sea 

i mourn the lack of mourning from the ones i've loved and i mourn the life i have not yet lost
I am mourning all this morning
the mourning that has yet to arrive
and i curse the dawn i cannot stop becoming

I think of the Sea and all the stories I have written 
I sit here in the sun and wonder why it rained so much tomorrow 
I sit hearing nothing but music and no fingers on my skin, my clothes 
I offered to my sister, 
with a caveat to lend them to whoever wanted, and to not expect them back 

The wretched beading sweat of my left leg stuck to Hesse's Truthful Game and tore off the front page 

The birds somewhere are singing;
And an organ plays, 
My anaesthetist plays the organ I was hearing 
And reads me silent breaths to sleep 
Telling me of all the keys he's had the right to touch 

In the dream, I was getting ice cream, with my sister 
We seemed to have reached some sort of resolution 
Once my dad had stood himself upon that same threshold, where I'd worried 
Borges would be able to escape 

And stood, silent, or with words dressed as silence 
Wearing lead vests, 
None of my radiation getting through 
I've never got through the little flap of skull that used to swing between us 

Lord, how i've tried to fit my body through the little hinge of mirror that was a kite stuck in a branch between us 
And my Sister reminds me how despair is just another self indulgence 
And I'm stuck here, in this sunshine on my toes as Borges scratches waking doors now leaving me, or selves I'd dreamed were being read the words of Aristotle to by my only sister 

I am scared to shit in case the only thing I can let go is blood 
All I've ever done is let go of the vital parts of me 
Holding onto waste 

And my toes are aching under some weight of light 
And I think of all the places I won't go 
And Seuss, and stories from my Mother when she angered at the stories I had tried to write with men 
Instead of women, 
And I think of all the words my sister read to me unceasing in my dream of Aristotle 
And I wondered then, while searching through this menu, 
Why all the ice cream sundaes took the forms of different animals 
And rabbits they appeared, and now I think about the rabbits left outside in hutches, reeking 
of sediment of shit and piss that she and I, my sister, had neglected 
Left to freeze and die in winters, in winters I've become in waking 
And I need to call my sister 
At least before next winter, should I make it 
And i think about the words I've tried to make into a husband 

And wonder if I have wished all of my weeping into being 

And wonder why it seems much harder that i did in fact wake up this morning 
as if my body is a heavy Poetics 
a stubbornness, 
A book of Aristotle 
Held up to hallway lighting, being read to me, awake 
Incessantly
By my sister
