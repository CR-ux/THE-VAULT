## LAST NIGHT'S DREAM

Good morning. How did you sleep?
What did you dream last night? 


I've been thinking a lot about why people find the retelling of others' dreams boring; because that's never been the case for me. Often one of the first things I'll ask someone I'm meeting for the first time is 'do you remember your dream last night?' or even just 'how often do you remember your dreams?'

Whether it's my experience with derealisation or the 'unreality' of the world around us or not, I think the dream world is no less real than the one you may find yourself in, listening to me say this, or perhaps waking up from your own dream.

Don't go back to sleep. People are going back and forth across the doorsill, where the two worlds touch. The door is round and open. Don't go back to sleep

Rumi's words were echoing in my head this morning as I woke from another nightmare 

It's probably clear that the original intent of the poet was to remind us that even through the inevitable cycles of experience and forgetting, progress and regression, it's important that we do our best to remember what we've been through, and remember that no matter how convincing the dream state of our emotional turmoil is, there was once, or can be again, a state of peace. 

In last nights nightmare, which seems to be a recurring one for me lately, I was being tortured and trying to escape from a concentration camp, having to resort to hideous violence against my captors in order to be free.

In this particular nightmare, I was using my pullsaw that I use to make the incisions and scarring in my paintings on reclaimed wood, to defend myself against a particularly terrifying warden, eventually stabbing and partially beheading him, as I was so used to the force required to cut through wood I had no idea of the flesh's softness and its relative fragility.

As is often the case in my dreams, I was somehow aware that I'd been here before, but just teetering on the edge of lucidity. These events were just one iteration of countless others just like it. And once I'd somehow managed to escape this endless horror, I realised the very people with whom I'd made my escape were more captors in disguise. I was alone, and I began the cycle again.

I don't recall how many permutations of this sequence I went through last night, and even less how many times I've had a nightmare just like this one. 

But I woke up. I woke up as always, still shuddering with this dread in my bones, wondering if the people I was running from were hiding in my wardrobe, if there really were monsters under my bed, ones that the child I was could never even dream of, that turned me into that child again. Nightmares that threatened, in a horrible mirror of Rumi's words 'don't go back to sleep'. 

But eventually, as always, I managed to steel myself, convince myself of the unreality of that place, that I was safe. That this dream wasn't a harbinger of things to come. 

Because it wasn't prophetic, it wasn't predicting a possible future. 
It's happening right now. 
There are people trapped in these cycles of violence right now, and they have been again, and again, for countless decades. 

There are children being bombed in their nursery. There are children being shown that the monsters aren't under their bed, they are hovering above them in jets, and hiding inside the governments they are too young to understand. They are in a nightmare, they are in a nightmare far more real than any of the ones we might have in the small seven hours we lie warm in our beds. They aren't sleeping, and the ones murdering them and their families are keeping them awake. Themselves not waking up. 

The door is round, this hateful circle. it is open. 
And I'm awake now. I'm awake now. Are you? 

What did you dream last night?
