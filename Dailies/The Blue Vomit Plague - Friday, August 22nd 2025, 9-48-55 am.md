## LAST NIGHT'S DREAM

in the dream, i was in my parents' bedroom again, with neither of them to be found.
In the opening sequence, there were a large number of dogs, the small kind. The fast kind. the uncontrollable kind. The unkind kind. 
One of the dogs had managed to insert its two sharpest claws into the soft soles of my feet. I remember waking in the night to use the bathroom, and checking my feet, wondering how my brain was capable of rendering such real, embodied tactile sense. It was one of the most intense instances of agony I've ever felt, in waking or in sleep. And that truly is saying something. Even as I write this, I still feel those long thorns, holding me fast in position, screaming, face down on the wooden floorboards, where I'd stubbed my toes on the nails sticking out countless times as a child. 
Once I was asleep again, I was lying next to a man, who was not my father.
We seemed to be alone in the house. 
When it was finished, I looked down at my torso to see patches of raw, red skin, blotched, ugly, sore. 
The man I was with tried to comfort me as the inflamed skin began to peel away, falling off in my hands like wood shavings to a chisel.
I found myself in hospital, being rushed past crowds of others, vomiting a viscous blue substance. 
I was wheeled on a gurney like it was a motorised vehicle; to an imaging suite. 
Where a doctor said to the imaging techs something about what was inside me -- ignoring my presence completely. 
She used personal pronouns, referring to the Thing, or Things, that were ravaging my body and peeling off its skin, as if they were conscious entities, bent upon escaping the vessel of my flesh. 
I began to beg, screaming for some sort of explanation -- it was my thought body. It was my body, I thought. I thought it was my body. 
The robotic spiderlegs of the imaging equipment whirred into action, and my body began to levitate above the gurney. There were intense zaps of electricity, barreling their way through my brain; as if the sensation gurgling and bubbling to the peripheral nerves underlaying the surface of my skin had been condensed, concentrated, and forced to emerge from the central nervous system. 

All the while, the doctor, gleefully observing with interest, was saying 'look. look. They are here.'


It became clear to me, somehow, even amidst the clinicians' total ignorance of my human life and my presence in the room, that I had become the first patient of this mysterious, blue-vomiting pandemic, that had presented with a symptomatology fulminant enough to be able to isolate the pathogen causing it. And in this more severe case, the pathogens, or life forms (by this time, I was sure it was plural) managed to circumvent the vomit response, and embed themselves deep enough to evade detection. 

I was placed in a chair. 
The chair began to wheel itself, even without wheels. 
I moved around the hospital, headed for some unknown, even more painful fate. 
I ended up in a swimming pool, realising that the host of my body was now entirely taken over by these creatures. 
The creatures wanted to continue their goal in secrecy. 
Inside the swimming pool arrived three others, subjected to the same fate. 
Each of us held a syringe of fluorescent yellow, bile-coloured solution. 
I watched as the three of them injected this fluid into their necks, and swam downwards, as far as they could, to drown. 

I watched my own arm emerge from the water, skin almost entirely gone, bone exposed, and I felt relief. I felt relief that I wouldn't have to look like this anymore. 

Once I had drowned next to this new family, who had held one another in death, at the bottom of that pool, I woke up once more in the hospital. 
This time, it was a newborn baby who had shown signs of the creatures on their skin. 
And I knew the creatures were iterating through this sequence, the population, finding the perfect host, whose immune system might not give them away. 

With this new insight, for each iteration of this cycle, the information of the creatures' existence perhaps left within me in error, I managed to conduct enough covert research to find out that it was the blue substance, the opaque, glossy vomit the world could no longer hold, that prevented the blossoming patches of skin from peeling from the body. 

I would begin collecting the vomit from the others, showing the doctor, who had, unbeknownst to her, once ignored the worth of my own life, that wiping this ejecta across the blemishes before the skin necrotised, reverted the skin back to health, at least in appearance. 

But perhaps the creatures allowed me this one, futile task, as distraction. Perhaps they gave me that knowledge, and that memory of each previous cycle, in order to prevent me knowing what their true motive was.


And now, each sentence I write is being punctuated 
By a drosophila, who will not-- no matter how many times I kick my ankle-- stop landing upon my foot. 
And I wonder
Are they here?
