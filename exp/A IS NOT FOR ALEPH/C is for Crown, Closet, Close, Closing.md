
### METACOMMENTARY 

NoetTuring --although being known primarily for his work in mathematics, computer science (the ancestor of our lexicomythographic field of Non-Ironic Byron{if|or}matics) and cryptAnalysis[^crypt] -- would practice poetry in his lonely evenings, although many of his verbose and decidedly amateurish works (especially when compared to his Right-Branched, publicly attested contributions to Science) were never read by a living soul. We respectfully dissect one said poem of this ineffably important queer paragon, who to many of us in the Society, rightly revere as nothing less than a God. As the following represents the first instance of publication of his poetic oeuvre, this article is tagged for internal dissemination only, until such time as the Schism's re-annealing, when the workings of our Society have been prophesied as being brought to light. 

### EDITOR'S NOET

This translation from the anteAntiquarian language of English into modern parlance appears to be just one of many correspondences to Turing's fellow Librarian, NoetSeuss, whose office in the Mythopoetic Playground of the Library has been found to be connected to Turing's Server Room-- via a one-way slide which led from the hexagonal ballpit of NoetSeuss's place of study, down to the basement. It is not yet known how, or if at all, Seuss's, or indeed Turing's traversal from the basement back to the ballpit was possible, but the method of written communication is evidently canonical - via mercurial psychopompic chimera under the infernal name of VIXI. Therefore, as ever, we cannot be certain of the biases and redactions present in the resultant love letter, which is clearly laden with expressions of grief; as many would be towards the end of the Polymathecule's romantic conjunction. It is, however, clear that the text underwent significant manipulations via its transmitter, the aforementioned carrier avian-reptile during its journey up the slide (made possible, of course, by VIXI's serpentine movement).
